### [#275](https://github.com/ocapn/ocapn/issues/275) Consensus on concrete encoding

JAR: Next would be 275: consensus on concrete encoding. Jessica added this.

Jessica: A few years ago we discussed concrete syntax and tentatively accepted Syrup as our current concrete syntax. I think this conversation was sparked by something Mark said at the last plenary and we talked about alternatives in the implementers meeting. We talked about CBOR and Preserves encoding. We came at this from a requirements POV in the implementers meeting and what we want from it. We have a proposal, maybe not a firm one, but it was Ridley's proposal. It's a custom encoding created just for OCapN that meets our needs. One reason to not use CBOR, my objection, is that while CBOR has a canonical representation it is optional and not the default in many implementations so you could easily get into a situation where an implementation would use non-canonical CBOR and break handoffs. CBOR has nice properties, it is compact and not human readable which is one of Mark's requirements. That's the context and why it's back on the agenda. I don't want to speak for Ridley, but maybe he would like to go through it.

Ridley: I had a few goals, mostly to meet the concerns expressed by the various parties at the time. It's a custom encoding so there is no way it can conflict with an existing encoding. It is inspired by the binary encoding of Preserves with some additional bits and pieces that I felt were important for OCapN. One of the things we were doing with the tentative Syrup encoding is that we were encoding all the types as Syrup records which ended up taking a lot of data in terms of byte count and what I propose is that we squash that down in this form and use a tag byte in front of the data, so that, say, op:deliver is just some single byte.  If your language allows pre-allocating arrays then this could be helpful. For the most part I tried to strike a balance between compactness and ease of implementation. I don't know if I got this exactly correct but I think ease of implementation is important and we don't want super complex bit twiddling. Kris and Richard had some feedback that was good. Richard wanted a different format for varints which I liked. Kris left some comments that I responded to but haven't heard back yet. He wanted some further optimization for integers. I am not proposing this as the exact encoding to adopt but I am proposing this as the direction we would like to go in knowing that there are bits that we have to change.

### [#142](https://github.com/ocapn/ocapn/issues/142) Error type and passage invariants

JAR: Objections to moving on to error types?  Issue 142.  This is an old one and it seems there hasn't been a lot of discussion.

MarkM: I took it on to add my requirements and preferences. I have something concrete in mind but I haven't written it down. Sorry about that.

JAR: I see you have a link to Endo's approach to errors. Should I assign you?

MarkM: Yes. Endo's current approach to errors is not what I'm currently advocating. I expect to do the engineering in Endo so Endo corresponds exactly to what I'll be proposing. I wanted to correct something I explained badly before. I was talking about making concessions for JS which did not reflect the initial conversation when this group formed. We have some concessions for JS, but we also have concessions for Spritely. If not for Spritely, we wouldn't have symbols. If not for Spritely we'd have method selectors for invocation rather than functions. JS and Spritely are sufficiently different from each other that coming up with things that make concessions for both is challenging, though both are dynamic languages which is why we've arrived at our OCapN design rather than the CapnProto design. For errors, I will be asking for some concessions that will help JS tremendously and unless other language have conflicts with the reservation of some specific names I don't think that it will cause the other languages much problem.

JAR: I'm looking for Spritely feedback on this.

Christine: First of all, I wanted to say that I appreciate the clarification Mark as I wasn't here at the last meeting. My recollection is that the initial goal is that if we can make Spritely and Agoric happy then we will be in a good place and that has been true. I'm very interested in what you're putting together Mark. We could really use some insights from active work and implementation. There's a question of if errors should have a specific shape or if we could leave it open-ended. Dave and I are interested in for distributed debugging and having ways of representing errors where not everyone can access it. For example, you could have sealed objects where you need an unsealer. The other is having the information cryptographically sealed. We haven't fully solved the distributed debugger space yet. We could use the tagged record type to allow for building more specific error representations but have the spec be open-ended, but i'm interested in your thoughts Mark.

MarkM: You're raising and important issue. I want to split the discussion in two parts. There's what the error information is carrying openly, what is unencrypted and openly transmitted over OCapN. That's the heart of what I'm proposing and the heart of what we need agreement on.  Then there's conventions built on top of the foundations. For example, the API surface of a lookup table, a map target (not a copy map), the API for a map target that would be natural for JS might not be natural for Spritely and vice versa, and that doesn't mean we need a compromise between the two.  It would be natural for JS to JS, but awkward for Spritely, and vice versa. I see the same thing for the public information on errors. What I'll be proposing is that the contents of errors are like the contents of structs, passable names with passable values, but because of security concerns I want to take seriously, I want to limit the values to "throwables", a subset of passable values that omits capabilities, that omits promises and targets. We often forget to be as vigilant as we should be about errors on the synchronous throw path and to a lesser extent on the reject/breakage path for promises. We often fail to be vigilant that these paths might convey capabilities. If the communication and the receiver/catcher want to communicate, I can't prevent the error path from communicating information that the intermediary may not like. We need to acknowledge that there's a problem for communicating information, but we're not threatening integrity if information leaks. The particular names I'm considering reserving from the struct, and it needs to be from the struct to be friendly to JS, are the names "name", "message", "stack", and in addition to the struct-like nature of an error, the other thing I want to transmit, not part of a struct, but an error id that addresses the private portion. The information that is not revealed in the error object itself. What's the information that requires rights amplification, say requiring a decryption key. The actual content of stacks would be part of this. The causality information leading up to an error, necessary for Causeway-like debugging, would also be part of the private information. A particular split that Endo has made that we can consider is that the contents of the message that is on the error can be a redacted form of the uncensored error that appears. What I'll be seeking agreement on is just an error id is that it's the notion that it's just an id that you have to push through some out-of-band rights amplification to get the private data. At this point I'm not looking to standardize anything else about private data.

Ridley: I want to clarify, I'm not in opposition to anything Mark is saying, I'm open to learning more, but an alternative perpsective we could take in Dart, JS, in Goblins, etc. is that you can break a promise with not just an error but anything.
